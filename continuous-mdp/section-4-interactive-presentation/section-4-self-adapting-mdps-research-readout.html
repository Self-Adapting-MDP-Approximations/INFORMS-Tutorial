Self-Adaptation in Continuous Markov Decision Processes

Two Key Bottlenecks

One LP, two infinities

Exact discounted-cost linear program

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{V:\,\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb R}\quad &\int_{\mathcal{S}}V(s)\,\nu(ds)\\ \text{s.t.}\quad &V(s)\le c(s,a)+\gamma\int_{\mathcal{S}}V(s')\,P(ds'\mid s,a), \qquad \forall s\in\mathcal{S},\ a\in\mathcal{A}_s. \end{aligned} \]

\(\infty\) constraints: every feasible \((s,a)\)
\(\infty\) columns: the full function \(V\)

1 · Manage the constraint bottleneck

Construct saddle-point model→ Optimize→ Refine saddle-point model

Constraint violation learning (CVL) uses primal–dual updates to adapt constraint sampling.

2 · Manage the approximation bottleneck

Construct value function approximation (VFA) → Optimize→ Refine VFA

Self-guided mechanism progressively refines the VFA and the linear program’s construction.

Start from the exact LP: every feasible state–action pair contributes a constraint, while the decision is the full value function. CVL addresses the constraint infinity by holding the basis fixed, constructing the regularized saddle-point model, and jointly optimizing the VFA coefficients and sampling density through PSMD. It has no separate Refine stage. Self-guided ALP addresses the column infinity by constructing random features, optimizing the enriched FALP, and refining with guiding constraints that preserve earlier VFA improvements.

Approximate Linear Program

Theory · §4.1

ALP Construction

  • Features or basis functions: features \(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_N:\mathcal{S}\mapsto\mathbb{R}\) define the VFA.

  • Weights: the coefficients \(\beta:=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_N)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\) define the VFA.

  • One constraint per pair: every feasible \((s,a)\) contributes a Bellman inequality.

  • Semi-infinite LP: finitely many coefficients, but infinitely many constraints when states or actions are continuous.

Finite-dimensional value function approximation (VFA)

\[ V_\beta(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta_n\varphi_n(s), \qquad \beta\in\mathbb R^{N+1} \]

Semi-infinite approximate linear program

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{\beta\in B}\quad & \mathbb E_\nu[V_\beta(s)] \\ \text{s.t.}\quad & V_\beta(s) \le c(s,a) +\gamma\,\mathbb E[V_\beta(S')\mid s,a], \quad \forall(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}_s. \end{aligned} \]

\(\mathcal{A}_s\) is the feasible action set

Basis functions compress the value function—but not the constraint set.

Walk down the three bullets before reading the program. The coefficient vector is finite, but feasibility must hold for every continuous state and every feasible action in the set A. That is the tension driving Section 4: the representation is compact, while finding the important constraints remains difficult.

Constraint violation function

Paper evidence · Figure 3

1Start from every ALP constraint

\[ V_\beta(s)\le c(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb E[V_\beta(S')\mid s,a], \qquad \forall(s,a). \]

2Use the quadratic VFA

\[ V_\beta(s)=\beta_0+\beta_1s+\beta_2s^2. \]

3Divide by \(1-\gamma>0\) and isolate the intercept

\[ \begin{aligned} (1-\gamma)\beta_0\le{}&c(s,a) -\beta_1\!\left(s-\gamma\mathbb E[S'\mid s,a]\right)\\[-2pt] &-\beta_2\!\left(s^2-\gamma\mathbb E[(S')^2\mid s,a]\right). \end{aligned} \]

\[ \beta_0\le f(\beta;s,a):= \frac{ \begin{aligned} c(s,a)&-\beta_1(s-\gamma\mathbb E[S'\mid s,a])\\[-2pt] &-\beta_2(s^2-\gamma\mathbb E[(S')^2\mid s,a]) \end{aligned} }{1-\gamma} \]

Begin with the Bellman inequality that appears once for every feasible state-action pair. Substitute the quadratic value approximation, collect the constant coefficient, and divide by one minus gamma, which is positive. The result shows that each pair imposes an upper threshold f on beta-zero. Darker, smaller values are more restrictive, and a point is violated when beta-zero exceeds that threshold. The orange point is only a grid minimum, not a proof of the continuous global minimum.

Regularized saddle point problem

Construct · CVL · equation 10

Define the feasible domain and admissible densities first

\[ D:=\{(s,a):s\in S,\ a\in \mathcal{A}_s\}, \qquad Y:=\left\{y\ge0:\ y\text{ continuous on }D,\ \int_Dy(s,a)\,d(s,a)=1\right\}. \]

1Eliminate the intercept

\[ \beta_0^* =\min_{(s,a)\in D}f(\beta;s,a), \]

\[ F=\max_{\beta\in B}\left\{\min_D f(\beta;s,a)+\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n\mathbb E_\nu[\varphi_n(s)]\right\}. \]

2Lift the search to \(Y\)

\[ \min_{(s,a)\in D}f(\beta;s,a) =\inf_{y\in Y}\mathbb E_y[f(\beta;s,a)]. \]

An average cannot beat the minimum; concentrating densities approach it.

3Regularize the density

\[ \mathbb E_y[f(\beta;s,a)]+\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(y\Vert p_u) \]

The uniform reference \(p_u\) discourages collapse onto a single pair.

\[ \boxed{ F(\lambda)=\max_{\beta\in B}\min_{y\in Y} \left\{\mathbb E_y[f(\beta;s,a)] +\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(y\Vert p_u)+\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n\mathbb E_\nu[\varphi_n(s)]\right\}.} \]

Construct step: replace nonconvex separation by optimization over a smooth, sampleable density.

Define the domain D and density set Y before using either. Step one eliminates the intercept because the ALP objective increases in beta-zero. Step two lifts the nonconvex point search to a linear expectation over densities without changing its value. Step three adds KL regularization relative to the uniform density. This is the construct step of CVL: it builds a sampleable alternative to repeatedly solving the global separation problem.

The optimal density is Gibbs

Theoretical result · Proposition 2

Fix \(\beta\in B\) and \(\lambda>0\)

\[ \min_{y\in Y} \left\{\mathbb E_y[f(\beta;s,a)] +\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(y\Vert p_u)\right\} \]

The minimum is attained at

\[ \boxed{ y^*_{\lambda,\beta}(s,a)= \frac{\exp[-f(\beta;s,a)/\lambda]} {\displaystyle\int_D\exp[-f(\beta;s',a')/\lambda]\,d(s',a')} } \]

The uniform reference density \(p_u\) cancels between numerator and denominator.

Key observations

  • KL strict convexity identifies the inner minimizer.
  • Smaller \(\lambda\) sharpens mass around minimizers of \(f\).
  • Metropolis–Hastings needs only relative density values.

The construct step now has a closed-form target density that can be sampled.

This is the theoretical bridge from the regularized saddle problem to a practical sampler. For fixed coefficients and positive lambda, the KL-regularized inner problem attains its optimum at the normalized Gibbs density. The first-order stationarity condition gives the exponential form, and the integral enforces unit mass. Because Metropolis-Hastings uses density ratios, the normalizing integral need not be computed explicitly.

Same target, different search

Constraint generation versus CVL

Hold \(\beta\) fixed

Exact separation

\[ \begin{aligned} (s_{\mathrm{CG}},a_{\mathrm{CG}}) &\in\arg\min_{(s,a)\in D}\{f(\beta;s,a)-\beta_0\}\\ &=\arg\min_{(s,a)\in D}f(\beta;s,a). \end{aligned} \]

Gibbs mode

\[ \boxed{\arg\max_D y^*_{\lambda,\beta} =\arg\min_D f(\beta;s,a).} \]

  • Constraint generation returns one worst pair.
  • CVL samples a neighborhood around the same target.
  • The identity holds for fixed \(\beta\) and every \(\lambda>0\).

Constraint generation searches for the mode; CVL turns that target into a sampling distribution.

This comparison is pointwise in beta. Exact separation minimizes f minus beta-zero, which has the same minimizers as f. Because the Gibbs exponential decreases strictly with f, its modes are exactly the minimizers of f. The methods do not follow the same coefficient trajectory: constraint generation adds one worst constraint, whereas CVL samples informative constraints around the same target region.

PSMD updates coefficients and density

Optimize · PSMD Algorithm 1

1Initialize and sample

\[ \beta^{(0)}=0,\quad y^{(0)}=p_u,\qquad (\widehat s_t,\widehat a_t)\sim y^{(t)},\quad \check s_t\sim P(\cdot\mid\widehat s_t,\widehat a_t). \]

The current density chooses the state–action constraint; the transition model supplies the next-state sample.

2Estimate the primal gradient

\[ \widehat L_t(\beta)= \frac{c(\widehat s_t,\widehat a_t) -\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n[\varphi_n(\widehat s_t)-\gamma\varphi_n(\check s_t)]} {1-\gamma} +\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n\mathbb E_\nu[\varphi_n(S)], \]

\[ \widehat g_t=\nabla_\beta\widehat L_t(\beta^{(t)}). \]

3Euclidean ascent for \(\beta\)

\[ \boxed{\beta^{(t+1)} =\Pi_B\!\left(\beta^{(t)}+\eta_t\widehat g_t\right).} \]

The maximizing variable updates the VFA coefficients and is projected back onto the compact set \(B\).

4KL mirror descent for \(y\)

\[ \boxed{ y^{(t+1)}(s,a)\propto [y^{(t)}(s,a)]^{\frac{1}{1+\eta_t\lambda_t}} \exp\!\left[ -\frac{\eta_t\widehat f_t(\beta^{(t)};s,a)} {1+\eta_t\lambda_t}\right].} \]

Decrease \(\eta_t\) and \(\lambda_t\) at order \(1/\sqrt{t}\), then form weighted averages \(\bar\beta\) and \(\bar y\).

Output: use the greedy cost-minimizing policy induced by \(V(\cdot;\bar\beta)\).

PSMD is primal-dual stochastic mirror descent on the regularized saddle problem. It draws a state-action pair from the current density and a next state from the transition law, giving unbiased first-order information for the maximizing coefficient vector. Beta uses Euclidean projected ascent. The minimizing density uses a KL mirror step, and both the step size and regularization parameter decrease at order one over square root t. The algorithm returns a weighted average coefficient vector and its greedy policy.

Value function approximation via random features

Construct · §4.3

So far: fixed basis functions

\[ \boldsymbol{\phi}(s)=\bigl(1,s,s^2\bigr), \qquad V_{\beta}(s)=\beta_0+\beta_1s+\beta_2s^2. \]

Next: random features

\[ \theta_n\overset{iid}{\sim}\vartheta,\qquad \varphi_n(s)=\phi\!\left(\theta_n^\top(1,s)\right), \qquad V_N(s;\beta)=\beta_0+\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n\varphi_n(s). \]

Each draw creates a fixed nonlinear basis; the ALP chooses its linear combination.

Contrast the fixed quadratic basis used in the inventory example with the random-feature approximation. Random features separate construction from fitting: their nonlinear parameters are sampled once from a prescribed distribution, and only their linear weights are decision variables. The three plots show Fourier, ReLU, and stump examples; change the number of features to see how the available approximation shapes become richer.

Feature-based ALP with continuous constraints

Construct + optimize · FALP

1Draw columns

Draw \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N\sim\vartheta\) and fix the random features \(\varphi_n\).

2Generate the VFA

\[ V_N(s;\beta)=\beta_0+\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_n\varphi_n(s). \]

3Keep every row

Impose one Bellman inequality for every feasible continuous pair \((s,a)\).

FALP\(_N\): random columns, continuous constraint set

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{\beta\in\mathbb R^{N+1}}\quad &\mathbb E_{\nu}[V_N(S;\beta)]\\ \text{s.t.}\quad &V_N(s;\beta) \le c(s,a)+\gamma\,\mathbb E[V_N(S';\beta)\mid s,a], \qquad \forall s\in\mathcal{S},\ a\in\mathcal{A}_s. \end{aligned} \]

Key convergence result:

Since we have \(V_N\le V^*\), the FALP optimal objective value \(\mathbb E_{\nu}[V_N]\) is a valid lower bound on the optimal policy cost \(\mathbb E_{\nu}[V^*]\).

\[ 0\le \mathbb E_{\nu}[V^*]-\mathbb E_{\nu}[V_N] \le \lVert V^*-V_N\rVert_{1,\nu} \le O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}) \]

Under some regularity and universal-feature assumptions, this convergence holds with high probability.

Randomness enters through the columns \(\theta_n\); the Bellman constraints still cover every state–action pair. In the inventory run, \(\nu\) is uniform.

FALP samples basis parameters, not Bellman constraints. Once the feature draw is fixed, the model is linear in beta, but it remains semi-infinite: the Bellman inequality must hold for every feasible state-action pair. The repository uses constraint sampling only as an implementation device; that numerical approximation is not part of the FALP definition on this slide.

FALP policy convergence caveat

What FALP controls

\[ \lVert V^*-V_N\rVert_{1,\nu} \longrightarrow 0. \]

A global approximation guarantee averaged under \(\nu\).

What the policy uses

\[ \begin{aligned} \pi^*(s)&\in\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A_s} \left\{c(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb E[V^*(S')\mid s,a]\right\}\\ \pi_N(s)&\in\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A_s} \left\{c(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb E[V_N(S')\mid s,a]\right\}. \end{aligned} \]

A local ranking of actions at each inventory state.

Improvement: \(\lVert V^*-V_N\rVert_{1,\nu} \ge \lVert V^*-V_{N+1}\rVert_{1,\nu}\) \(\quad\nRightarrow\quad\) Not guaranteed: \(\mathrm{PV}_\chi(\pi_{N+1})\le \mathrm{PV}_\chi(\pi_N)\)

The FALP results ensure VFA and lower bouund improvements, but not monotone improvement of the greedy-policy upper bound.

This is a mechanism, not a numerical claim about a particular random-feature draw. In the inventory problem, the greedy policy compares order quantities through their one-step cost plus approximate continuation value. Near a reorder threshold, the best two actions have a small true action gap. Even when the FALP objective and the value approximation converge on average under the state-relevance distribution, localized continuation-value errors can change the sign of that gap and move the threshold. Thus the exact policy cost \(J_\nu(\pi_N)\) is an upper bound for this cost-minimization problem, but the FALP convergence theorem alone does not make those upper bounds monotone. This does not say the policies can never converge; it says that lower-bound convergence by itself is insufficient.

Self-guided ALP

Construct + optimize + refine · equation 12

Construct: add \(B\) features→ Optimize: solve FALP\(_N\)→ Refine: retain the statewise floor↺

At round \(q\), \(N=qB\) and the previous VFA becomes a guide

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{\beta\in\mathbb R^{N+1}}\quad &\mathbb E_{\nu}[V_N(S;\beta)]\\ \text{s.t.}\quad &V_N(s;\beta)\le c(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb E[V_N(S';\beta)\mid s,a], \quad\forall s\in\mathcal{S},\ a\in\mathcal{A}_s,\\ &\boxed{V_N(s;\beta)\ge V_{N-B}(s;\beta^{\mathrm{SG}}_{N-B}),\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.} \end{aligned} \]

Why the guide matters

  • Memory: the new lower bound cannot fall below the previous one.
  • Signal: a dual multiplier \(y_N^*(s)\) can be positive only where the guide binds.
  • Reweighting: those shadow prices induce an effective objective distribution.

Equivalent state relevance after the solve

\[ \nu'_N(s)= \frac{\nu(s)+y_N^*(s)} {1+\int_{\mathcal{S}}y_N^*(u)\,du}. \]

Self-guided ALP improves \(\lVert V^*-V_N\rVert_{1,\nu} \ge \lVert V^*-V_{N+1}\rVert_{1,\nu}\) only if \(\lVert V^*-V_N\rVert_{\infty} \ge \lVert V^*-V_{N+1}\rVert_{\infty}\)

Self-guided ALP guarantees improving worst-case policy performance from \(N\) to \(N+1\)

This is the complete construct–optimize–refine loop. Construct samples a new batch of columns. Optimize solves the enriched FALP. Refine carries the earlier VFA forward as a statewise floor. Appending zero coefficients preserves the previous feasible solution. The dual interpretation is ex post: the self-guided optimum is equivalent to a FALP whose state-relevance distribution is the normalized sum of the original distribution and the guiding-constraint dual density. Complementarity means extra weight can occur only where the guide is tight.
